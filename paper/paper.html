Abstract

Research Summary

This study asks whether page-level search-performance signals can support the prioritization of content for refresh review. The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse and frames refresh prioritization as a supervised decision-support problem. A leakage-safe Random Forest classifier was evaluated against a majority-class baseline using a held-out stratified evaluation split. Because the target is imbalanced at approximately 8% positive observations, the baseline achieved higher raw accuracy (0.919 versus 0.897), while the model provided non-zero precision, recall, and F1 (0.194, 0.086, and 0.120) on the minority outcome. The result is therefore best interpreted as a weak but measurable decision-support signal rather than evidence that a model can guarantee future rankings, traffic, clicks, or content-refresh improvements.

01 · Research Question

Question & Decision Supported

Research Question
Can page-level search-performance signals be used to identify content that should be prioritized for refresh before its search visibility deteriorates?

Decision Supported

The analysis supports a content-refresh prioritization decision: which pages should be reviewed first based on measurable performance signals rather than relying only on manual judgment.

The objective is deliberately operational. The model is intended to help allocate limited editorial attention; it is not intended to replace editorial expertise or automatically modify content.

03 · Methodology

Modeling & Evaluation Design

Problem Formulation

Content-refresh prioritization is treated as a supervised classification problem. The objective is not to predict search rankings directly, but to identify observations associated with the predefined decision-support target.

Assumptions

The methodology assumes that historical page-level search-performance signals contain information that may be useful for prioritizing pages for human review.

This is a decision-support exercise rather than a causal estimate of the effect of refreshing content.

Features

Candidate features represent observable page-level search-performance and engagement signals. Feature construction was designed to avoid variables that directly reveal the target.

Label Definition

The target represents whether an observation meets the predefined priority condition established during the capstone workflow. The label is treated as a decision-support outcome rather than a ground-truth measurement of whether a future refresh will succeed.

Baseline

A majority-class baseline provides a simple reference point. Both the baseline and machine-learning model are evaluated on the same held-out observations.

Model

The final leakage-safe model is a Random Forest classifier. The model was selected as a practical nonlinear baseline capable of representing interactions between page-level performance signals without requiring a highly complex architecture.

Validation Design

The final evaluation uses unseen observations through an 80/20 stratified split. This ensures that the reported model metrics are calculated on observations that were not used for model fitting.

Leakage Audit

Important methodological finding: An earlier version of the workflow included gsc_clicks and CTR-derived information even though the target itself was defined using click information. This created target leakage and artificially inflated apparent model performance.

The leakage-affected configuration was not retained as the final model. The leakage-safe evaluation excludes variables that directly define or inherit the target.

Target: gsc_clicks_gt_0 Leakage-control exclusions: - gsc_clicks → directly used to construct the target - ctr → derived from gsc_clicks / gsc_impressions - client_hash_id → identifier and public-safety exclusion - content_hash_id → identifier and public-safety exclusion Validation: 80/20 stratified held-out evaluation Interpretation: Decision support, not causal prediction.

How the Result Should Be Read

The baseline achieves higher raw accuracy because the target is heavily imbalanced. With approximately 8% positive observations, a majority-class classifier can achieve high accuracy simply by predicting the majority class for every observation.

That strategy, however, has zero recall for the minority outcome. The leakage-safe Random Forest sacrifices some overall accuracy while producing non-zero precision, recall, and F1.

Main Finding

The majority-class baseline outperformed the leakage-safe model on raw accuracy (0.919 versus 0.897), but accuracy alone is not sufficient for interpreting this imbalanced problem. The model provides a weak, non-zero signal for identifying minority-class observations that the majority baseline cannot identify.

With recall of 0.086, the model detects only a limited fraction of the positive cases. With precision of 0.194, most flagged observations are not positive under the selected label. Consequently, the model should be treated as a prioritization aid rather than a high-confidence prediction system.

Methodological Lesson

The most important result is not the absolute accuracy figure. The leakage audit demonstrated that an apparently strong model can be misleading when target-defining information is supplied as a feature. Removing those variables produced a substantially more conservative and defensible evaluation.

05 · Limitations

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The target is a decision-support label rather than a directly observed future business outcome.
  • A high-priority score does not establish that refreshing a page will improve rankings, clicks, or traffic.
  • Observational search-performance data do not establish causality.
  • Results may not generalize to every website, industry, search environment, or future reporting period.
  • Performance depends on feature selection, target construction, validation design, and the evaluation period.
  • The current model has limited recall and precision and should therefore not be treated as an autonomous decision system.
  • Additional features, class-weighting strategies, threshold optimization, and alternative target definitions may improve future versions.
  • The earlier leakage-affected configuration demonstrates why leakage controls are essential to trustworthy machine-learning evaluation.
Strongest Defensible Claim

This analysis provides a reproducible, data-informed framework for prioritizing content for human review. It does not establish that the model can guarantee future search-performance improvements or that refreshing a page causes better rankings, clicks, or traffic.

06 · Action Playbook

Ranked Recommendations

Model output should be translated into an accountable workflow. Rankings are intended to determine where human attention should be allocated first.

01

Review the highest-priority pages first

Start with pages receiving the strongest refresh-priority signals. Review recent performance, content quality, freshness, and search-intent alignment before making an intervention.

02

Investigate sustained performance declines

Distinguish persistent deterioration from temporary fluctuations by comparing recent performance with an appropriate historical period.

03

Prioritize actionable opportunities

Give greater operational attention to cases where the diagnosis indicates a concrete intervention, such as outdated information, incomplete coverage, weak intent alignment, or unclear content structure.

04

Require human review

The model is a prioritization mechanism, not an autonomous content editor. Human reviewers should validate recommendations before changes are published.

05

Measure after refresh

Track subsequent performance against an appropriate pre-refresh period while avoiding unsupported causal claims.

Detect → Diagnose → Prioritize → Human Review → Refresh → Measure

Final Takeaway

From Prediction to Responsible Decision Support

The central lesson of this project is that useful machine-learning work requires more than training a model. A trustworthy workflow must define the decision clearly, establish a baseline, prevent target leakage, evaluate on unseen data, report limitations, and translate model output into an accountable operational process.

In this study, the leakage-safe model did not outperform the majority baseline on raw accuracy. Nevertheless, it produced a non-zero signal for the minority outcome that the baseline could not detect. That result is modest, but it is more informative than an inflated score produced by leakage.

The resulting framework is therefore best understood as a structured content-review prioritization system: Detect → Diagnose → Prioritize → Human review → Refresh → Measure.
